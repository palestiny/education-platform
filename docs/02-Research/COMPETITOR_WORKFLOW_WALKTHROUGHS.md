# Competitor Workflow Walkthroughs

Status: Research Draft

Gate: Product Foundation / Competitive Intelligence — NOT PROVEN

Implementation is not authorized by this document.

## Scope

This document records public evidence about how selected competitors connect users, learning activity, evidence, and follow-up actions. It does not claim hands-on product testing.

Evidence is classified as:
- VERIFIED PUBLIC CAPABILITY — documented by the provider.
- USER REPORT — reported by users/reviewers and not independently verified.
- OPEN QUESTION — requires hands-on validation or additional evidence.

## Workflow A — Khan Academy / Khan Academy Kids

### Student

Public evidence indicates:
1. Student enters a learning experience with standards-aligned lessons and adaptive/personalized learning paths.
2. Student completes lessons/practice activities.
3. The platform records progress and mastery-related information.
4. Teacher-facing tools can expose where students are and what they need next.

Classification: VERIFIED PUBLIC CAPABILITY.

### Teacher

Public evidence indicates:
1. Teacher creates a class.
2. Teacher assigns lessons by target skill/standard.
3. Students complete assigned or personalized-path work.
4. Teacher views individual or class progress and score history.
5. Teacher uses the resulting information to support instruction.

Classification: VERIFIED PUBLIC CAPABILITY.

### Parent / Caregiver

Public evidence indicates:
1. Parent can access a parent-oriented progress view.
2. Parent can see what the child has been working on.
3. In Khan Academy Kids, parent and teacher/class workflows can coexist around the same learner profile; the provider documents conversion from Parent Account to Class Account while retaining progress.

Classification: VERIFIED PUBLIC CAPABILITY.

### Research implication

Khan Academy demonstrates that student learning, teacher assignment/measurement, and parent visibility can be connected in one ecosystem. This means those individual capabilities cannot by themselves define differentiation for our product.

Sources checked: Khan Academy teacher and Khan Academy Kids public documentation (2026).

## Workflow B — Classera

### Organization / Teacher / Student

Public product material describes an ecosystem spanning learning management, assessment, AI-supported personalization, analytics/dashboard capabilities, and broader institutional services.

Classification: VERIFIED PUBLIC CAPABILITY based on provider documentation.

### Research implication

The market already contains broad institutional platforms. Therefore, "many features in one platform" is not sufficient as a validated product distinction.

Open question: hands-on testing is still required to determine whether evidence actually flows coherently from learner state to teacher prioritization, intervention, and follow-up in day-to-day use.

## Workflow C — Abwaab

Public provider material describes curriculum explanations, a question bank, teacher Q&A, and live classes. A 2026 Classera announcement also describes an AI recommendation capability intended to connect student data, interests, academic level, and content/course recommendations through the Classera-Abwaab partnership.

Classification: VERIFIED PUBLIC CAPABILITY.

Research implication: recommendation/personalization is already an active market direction. Our differentiation hypothesis therefore needs to be validated at the workflow level, not by simply adding AI recommendations.

## Workflow D — Nagwa

Public product material confirms curriculum-oriented educational content for the Egyptian market.

Classification: VERIFIED PUBLIC CAPABILITY.

Open question: the current evidence set is not sufficient to claim how effectively learner evidence, parent visibility, teacher intervention, and organizational workflows are connected end-to-end.

## Cross-competitor observation

Current public evidence supports the following factual conclusion:

Existing products already cover substantial parts of:
- content delivery;
- practice and assessment;
- teacher progress views;
- parent visibility in at least some products;
- institutional reporting;
- AI/personalization in parts of the market.

Therefore the working research question remains:

> Can we coordinate evidence, interpretation, decision, intervention, and follow-up across student, parent, teacher, and organization without increasing user complexity?

This remains a PRODUCT HYPOTHESIS, not a requirement.

## Validation gaps

The following remain NOT PROVEN:
1. Whether users actually experience the above workflows as coherent rather than fragmented.
2. Whether parents can move from progress information to an appropriate action without surveillance overload.
3. Whether teachers can reliably identify who needs attention, why, and what intervention is appropriate.
4. Whether organizations can coordinate exceptions and interventions without manual cross-system work.
5. Whether recommendation quality can be grounded in sufficient evidence.
6. How the workflows behave under failure, delay, missing data, connectivity problems, and support escalation.
7. Which workflow elements must remain human-controlled.
