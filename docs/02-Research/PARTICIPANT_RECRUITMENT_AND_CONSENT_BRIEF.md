# Participant Recruitment and Consent Brief

**Status:** Research Instrument  
**Gate:** Product Foundation / Competitive Intelligence — NOT PROVEN  
**Implementation authorization:** None

## Purpose

This brief prepares ethical, neutral recruitment for direct validation of real educational intervention workflows.

The research is intended to understand how a recent case was handled across evidence, interpretation, decision ownership, action, follow-up, outcome and recovery. It is **not** intended to sell a product, validate a proposed feature, evaluate a teacher/parent/student, or collect unnecessary personal information.

## Research hypothesis under test

A recurring intervention case may become harder to manage when evidence, interpretation, decision ownership, intervention, follow-up and outcome evidence are distributed across people, tools or channels.

This is a hypothesis. Recruitment language must not present it as an established problem.

## Initial participant groups

Recruit across one or more of:

- private tutors;
- tutoring-center teachers or coordinators;
- parents/guardians;
- students where age, consent and safeguarding requirements permit.

The initial segment remains unvalidated. Recruitment should seek real cases rather than forcing a preferred segment.

## Participant profile

Prefer participants who can describe a **recent real case** involving one or more of:

- repeated learning difficulty;
- attendance plus performance concern;
- missing work or overdue support;
- parent concern;
- remediation and reassessment;
- an unresolved case requiring coordination between roles.

A participant who has no suitable recent case is still useful as a negative finding, but the interview must not be replaced with a hypothetical scenario and must be recorded as such.

## Neutral recruitment message

> We are conducting research on how educational situations are handled in real life, especially when a student needs follow-up or support. We are interested in a recent real example and the steps people took, including what information they used, who made decisions, how follow-up happened, and how they knew whether the situation was resolved.
>
> This is research, not a sales call and not an evaluation of your performance. We will not ask you to test a product. Participation is voluntary, and you may skip any question.
>
> We prefer not to collect names, student identifiers, grades, contact details, private messages, credentials, or other sensitive information unless it is genuinely necessary for the research question.

## Before the session

Confirm:

- participant understands the research purpose;
- participation is voluntary;
- participant may stop or skip questions;
- no academic, employment, financial or service decision depends on participation;
- the interviewer will avoid unnecessary sensitive information;
- screenshots, records or messages will only be reviewed with explicit permission;
- any shared artifact will be minimized/redacted before repository storage;
- no credentials, authentication codes, private keys or account access will be requested;
- minors require an appropriate consent/assent process and safeguarding review before participation.

## Artifact handling

The preferred evidence hierarchy is:

1. direct observation of the workflow, when ethically and practically possible;
2. participant-described facts;
3. permitted/redacted artifacts;
4. researcher interpretation.

Do not store raw private conversations, student records, photographs, account exports or other unnecessary sensitive material in the repository.

Use case IDs such as `CASE-0002`; do not use participant names.

If an artifact cannot be safely minimized, do not copy it into the repository. Record only the relevant factual observation.

## Interviewer conduct

The interviewer must:

- ask what actually happened before discussing possible solutions;
- avoid leading questions;
- preserve contradictory evidence;
- distinguish what was observed from what the participant believes;
- ask how the participant knows an outcome occurred;
- ask what already worked well;
- ask what failed and how recovery occurred;
- avoid implying that a product should replace human judgment;
- avoid promising a future feature or product.

## Minimum case evidence

A usable case should attempt to capture:

- trigger;
- evidence and evidence quality;
- interpretation and uncertainty;
- decision owner;
- action owner;
- intervention;
- communication and context handoff;
- due state;
- follow-up;
- outcome evidence;
- closure;
- current workaround;
- cost/friction;
- failure/recovery;
- privacy/consent;
- AI/human accountability when relevant;
- contradictory evidence.

## Research stop conditions

Do not manufacture evidence to fill gaps.

Stop or reframe the research if:

- participants provide only hypothetical opinions;
- the workflow does not actually require cross-role coordination;
- the current tools close the loop with little friction;
- the problem is materially different from the hypothesis;
- privacy or safeguarding cannot be handled responsibly;
- evidence remains too weak to support a product decision.

## Gate boundary

Recruitment readiness does **not** mean the hypothesis is validated.

The Product Foundation Gate remains **NOT PROVEN** until real cases are collected, contradictory evidence is reviewed, and the criteria in `DIRECT_VALIDATION_GATING_CHECKLIST.md` are explicitly assessed.

Passing this gate would only authorize progression into requirements discovery. It would not authorize architecture or implementation.
