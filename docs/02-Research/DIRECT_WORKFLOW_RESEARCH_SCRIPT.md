# Direct Workflow Research Script

Status: Research Instrument  
Gate: Product Foundation / Competitive Intelligence — NOT PROVEN  
Implementation authorization: None

## Purpose

Provide a neutral, repeatable interview protocol for reconstructing real recent intervention cases. The objective is to discover what actually happened, not to validate a proposed product or collect feature requests.

## Research rules

- Ask about a real recent case whenever possible.
- Prefer "tell me what happened" over "would you use".
- Do not suggest the proposed workflow before the participant describes the current workflow.
- Record facts, participant reports, artifacts, and researcher interpretation separately.
- Never invent missing details.
- Avoid unnecessary personal or student-identifying information.
- Ask permission before reviewing screenshots, messages, records, or other artifacts.
- Do not collect sensitive information unless necessary for the research question.
- Do not imply that a problem exists because the platform could solve it.
- Contradictory evidence must be recorded.

## Opening

Explain that the discussion is about how a real educational situation was handled, not about evaluating the participant or testing their performance.

Ask for a recent case involving one of:
- repeated learning difficulty;
- attendance plus performance concern;
- missing work or overdue support;
- parent concern;
- remediation/reassessment;
- an unresolved case requiring coordination between roles.

If no real case is available, record that explicitly and do not replace it with a hypothetical scenario.

## Case reconstruction

### 1. Trigger

- What happened?
- When did you first notice something required attention?
- What made it different from an ordinary situation?

### 2. Evidence

- What was the first evidence?
- Where did that evidence come from?
- What other evidence did you check?
- Was any evidence missing, stale, contradictory, or difficult to interpret?
- Could you verify the evidence?

### 3. Interpretation

- What did you think was happening?
- What alternatives did you consider?
- What were you uncertain about?
- Who else helped interpret the situation?

### 4. Decision ownership

- Who decided that action was needed?
- Who was responsible for deciding what to do?
- Was ownership clear?
- Did anyone assume someone else was handling it?

### 5. Action ownership

- Who actually carried out the action?
- What exactly was done?
- Was the action different from the original plan?

### 6. Communication and context

- Who needed to know?
- How was the situation communicated?
- Which tool or channel was used?
- Did the context survive when it moved between people or tools?
- Did anyone have to repeat information?

### 7. Due state and follow-up

- Was there a specific next step or due date?
- Who was expected to remember it?
- How was the follow-up tracked?
- What happened if nobody followed up?

### 8. Outcome and closure

- How did you determine whether the intervention worked?
- What evidence showed improvement, no change, or uncertainty?
- Was reassessment performed?
- Who decided the case was closed?
- Could the case remain open without anyone noticing?

### 9. Workaround and cost

- What tools were used: platform, WhatsApp, calls, paper, spreadsheet, calendar, memory, or something else?
- How many handoffs were involved?
- What had to be entered or communicated more than once?
- What was the most time-consuming part?
- What happened when the normal tool failed or was unavailable?

### 10. Privacy and consent

- Who was allowed to see the relevant information?
- Was any information intentionally withheld?
- Were there concerns about parent visibility, student autonomy, or teacher workload?
- Was consent required for any part of the process?

### 11. AI and human accountability

Only ask if AI or automated recommendations were actually involved.

- What did the system recommend or detect?
- What evidence supported it?
- Who reviewed the recommendation?
- Who could reject or modify it?
- What happened when the recommendation was wrong, uncertain, or unsupported?

## Closing questions

- What part of this case was hardest to manage?
- What part was already handled well by your current tools?
- If the same situation happened again, what would you do differently?
- What evidence would you need to trust a recommendation or alert?
- What should a system never decide or communicate automatically?

## Researcher capture

Immediately after the interview, record:

1. Trigger
2. Evidence and evidence quality
3. Interpretation and uncertainty
4. Decision owner
5. Action owner
6. Intervention
7. Communication/context preservation
8. Due state
9. Follow-up
10. Outcome evidence
11. Closure
12. Workaround
13. Cost/friction
14. Failure/recovery
15. Privacy/consent
16. AI/human boundary
17. Contradictory evidence
18. Open questions

## Anti-leading checks

Before accepting the case as evidence, verify:

- The participant described a real case.
- The interviewer did not introduce the proposed solution first.
- The participant's current workaround is recorded.
- The existence of a problem is not inferred solely from a missing feature.
- Positive evidence and negative evidence are both recorded.
- One case is treated as one case, not as prevalence.

## Evidence classification

Use:
- DIRECT OBSERVATION
- USER REPORT
- ARTIFACT EVIDENCE
- MARKET EVIDENCE
- INTERPRETATION
- HYPOTHESIS

A participant statement remains a USER REPORT unless independently supported by an artifact or direct observation.
