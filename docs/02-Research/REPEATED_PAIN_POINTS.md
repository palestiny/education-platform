# Repeated Pain Points and User Needs

Date: 2026-09-24
Status: Research Draft

## Evidence rule

A complaint becomes a candidate repeated pattern only when multiple independent sources support materially similar friction. A single complaint is recorded as a USER REPORT, not a systemic market weakness.

## Current evidence

### P-001 — Parents need interpretable progress, not only raw activity

Evidence status: RESEARCH HYPOTHESIS / NOT PROVEN

Khan Academy demonstrates that parent visibility is a mature product area: parents can view activity, assignments, progress reports, and learner information.

The open question is whether parents consistently understand:
- what matters now;
- why the child is struggling;
- what action is appropriate;
- when teacher intervention is needed;
- whether improvement is occurring.

This requires user research before becoming a requirement.

### P-002 — Education platforms can optimize for digital activity without proving learning impact

Evidence status: VERIFIED MARKET CONCERN

UNESCO reports that robust evidence on the added value of education technology remains limited, and emphasizes learning outcomes over digital inputs. It also notes mixed evidence and context dependence.

This supports a product-quality requirement candidate:

Measure learning evidence and outcomes, not only clicks, minutes, views, or completion events.

This is a research-backed principle candidate, not yet a final metric specification.

### P-003 — Access and context can affect whether digital education works

Evidence status: VERIFIED MARKET CONCERN

UNESCO reports substantial connectivity/access inequalities and stresses that technology decisions should be appropriate, equitable, scalable, and sustainable.

This creates design questions around:
- low-bandwidth use;
- mobile-first access;
- offline/hybrid learning;
- accessibility;
- device constraints;
- continuity when connectivity fails.

These are not implementation requirements yet; they need prioritization during Requirements and UX Gates.

### P-004 — Reliability, access and navigation failures can interrupt learning

Evidence status: EARLY PATTERN / NOT PROVEN SYSTEMIC

Public reviews across multiple platforms contain materially similar reports involving app crashes/freezing, login/logout problems, missing or mismatched curriculum/grade options, playback/loading issues, and navigation/usability friction. Current evidence includes examples from Noon Academy, Abwaab and Khan Academy.

The correct conclusion at this stage is **early pattern signal**, not “market-wide systemic weakness.” Frequency, severity, affected segments, and persistence require structured sampling.

Research questions:
- How often do these failures occur?
- Which user segments are most affected?
- Does the failure block learning or merely add friction?
- Is recovery clear and fast?
- Can the learner resume without losing context?
- Is support continuity adequate when self-recovery fails?

## Patterns not yet proven

The following hypotheses remain OPEN and require direct user research or stronger independent evidence:

- Parents receive data but still cannot quickly answer what should I do now?
- Students struggle more with choosing the next action than finding content.
- Teachers lose substantial time to fragmented administrative workflows.
- Learning history is fragmented across lessons, practice, exams, private tutoring, and offline sessions.
- Existing platforms provide analytics but insufficiently convert evidence into coordinated interventions.
- Parents want confidence and actionable summaries rather than surveillance-heavy monitoring.
- Centers need a unified operating layer across teaching, attendance, assessment, communication, and finance.
- Reliability/recovery quality materially affects learning outcomes or retention at meaningful scale.

## Research conclusion

The evidence is now strong enough to promote reliability/access/navigation friction from isolated examples to an **EARLY PATTERN signal**, but not enough to declare a market-wide weakness.

The next step is structured evidence collection: dated review sampling, user interviews, surveys, support/review analysis, and competitor workflow walkthroughs.
